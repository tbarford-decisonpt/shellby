### New
- **Up to twelve conversations side by side.** Split (Ctrl+\) or drag tabs in, up to four columns of three. A 4×1 strip across a wide screen works too. Drag the line between two panes to resize them, and double-click it to even them out. The panel grows to fit when it has to, and doesn't shrink back.
- **Tabs in every pane.** Each pane has its own tab strip and can hold several conversations, like editor groups in VS Code. Drop a tab on a pane's strip to put it there, on the middle of a pane to add it at the end, or on an edge to give it a pane of its own. A pane's × moves its conversations into the pane beside it, so nothing closes by accident. With one pane the strip stays at the top as before. While split, the review and every-conversation buttons sit at the end of the bar under the strip.
- **A box in every pane.** The pane you're in has the message box. The others show what you've typed in each, and a click or a key carries on there.
- **Alt+arrows** go from pane to pane. **Ctrl+Alt+arrows** move a conversation into the pane beside it, and at the left or right edge into a column of its own. Ctrl+Tab and Ctrl+PgUp/PgDn go through the tabs of the pane you're in, and Ctrl+Shift+PgUp/PgDn move a tab along its strip.

### Changed
- **The split view comes back after a restart**, with the same panes, the same tabs in each and the same sizes, if it was split when you quit. Pop-outs aren't saved and come back into the panel.
